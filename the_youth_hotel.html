🔵 PART I — THE FOUR DIMENSIONS ARE EQUIVALENT

I.1 — The Illusion of Difference

We live in a four-dimensional world. Physicists distinguish between time and the three spatial dimensions — but that distinction is not fundamental. It is produced.

There is no fundamental difference between the four dimensions. The differences we observe are produced by mass-energy distribution, which creates the differences we perceive: up-down, front-back, left-right, and before-after.

The most obvious is up-down. This spatial dimension is created by the curvature produced by mass-energy distribution — but it is a dynamic situation: matter creates up-down and simultaneously tends downward, while energy tends upward.

I.2 — The Rotations We Already Know

Humans navigate spatial dimensions freely. A 90° rotation exchanges left-right for front-back. Lying down exchanges up-down for front-back.

But there is a crucial difference always overlooked: to ascend or descend we need support. Without support we fall. An astronaut floating in the centre of their spacecraft cannot rotate left-right without reaching a surface.

The question nobody asks: if spatial dimensions are only separated by right angles, is it impossible to exchange a spatial dimension for the temporal one? Physicists say enormous quantities of dark energy would be required. But in quantum mechanics, that is not the case.
✅ PART II — THE HADAMARD GATE AND TEMPORAL EXCHANGE

II.1 — What Hadamard Does

In quantum computing, the Hadamard gate performs a 180° rotation around an axis in the bisector of two spatial dimensions ZX. This strange rotation involves the temporal dimension.

Physicists say applying Hadamard to a qubit places it in superposition: the particle is simultaneously in a state and its opposite until measured, at which point it "collapses" into one.

New interpretation: Quantum superposition is not a mystery. When a spatial dimension exchanges with the temporal one, the particle's complete history is presented along a spatial length. Collapse is simply the return of the temporal and spatial dimensions to their everyday configuration. The value is superposed because it comes from the future and mixes with the past.

II.2 — The Mirror as Everyday Example

In a mirror, up-down does not change. Left-right does not change either — despite appearances. Only front-back changes. And time.

The person we see in the mirror is real. It is not our reflection: it is us living life in reverse, from old age toward youth.

Rotations always involve two dimensions. A rotation involving only one spatial dimension means the other dimension involved, not being any of the other two spatial ones, must necessarily be time.
