The Two Waves

Same catastrophe. Two groups of survivors. One coherent tradition — and one scattered into myth.

Younger Dryas · Shemsu-Hor · Declared Speculation

This essay builds on "The War of the Gods" — read that first for the destruction of Quinta and the Ark of Secundus. What follows is declared speculation: coherent with the evidence, not proven by it.

Two groups of survivors reached Earth after the same catastrophe — the Younger Dryas impact event, c. 12,900 BC — but in radically different conditions. That difference explains why only one tradition survived as a coherent narrative, and why the other dissolved into scattered, fragmentary myth across the world.

I
The First Wave — Survivors of Quinta

When Quinta was destroyed, its stellar fleet was left in orbit with no home planet and no fuel to leave the solar system. The only option was descent — uncontrolled, with too few evacuation craft for everyone aboard. Ships fell in a controlled but desperate manner; crews jumped at the last possible moment, scattering across the planet with whatever they carried.

What resulted was total dispersion. No coordination between survivors. No intact chain of command. No heavy technology — it had been left behind in the ships that continued falling, empty, after the crews jumped.

Memory fragmented quickly. Within a few generations, all that remained was the recollection of beings who had fallen from the sky — without a coherent origin story, without genealogy, without explanation. Viracocha emerging from Lake Titicaca. Oannes emerging from the sea. The Nommo descending in their celestial ark. Isolated figures, each remembered locally, never connected into a single narrative.

II
The Second Wave — Survivors of the Ark of Secundus

The Ark of Secundus met a different fate. Too large to land, it was evacuated before impact — not an emergency fall, but an executed plan. Leadership remained intact. Noah commanded the operation. The chain of command did not break.

Because leadership survived, so did memory. This group knew where they came from, what had happened, and why. Their arrival was not scattered but concentrated — a directed landing toward the Sahara, then green and fertile, rather than a random fall across the globe.

The result: a coherent flood narrative, with genealogy, chronology and purpose — the tradition that survives today as the story of Noah's Ark, told and retold with internal consistency across Sumerian, biblical and other texts.

III
The Meeting — Shemsu-Hor

At some point after the impact, the two waves met in the Sahara — then a green, hospitable land, not yet desert. The Turin King List records what followed: the Shemsu-Hor, "Followers of Horus," ruling before Menes, the first mortal pharaoh (c. 3,100 BC).

One possible reading: the organised wave — the Ark's survivors, with intact leadership and memory — assumed command. The scattered wave — Quinta's survivors, without structure of their own — integrated as the base population. This would explain Egypt's earliest social hierarchy: a ruling elite with intact memory governing a population whose memory had already begun to fragment.

IV
A Chronological Correction — What the Shemsu-Hor Actually Ruled

The Turin Papyrus gives a precise figure: the Shemsu-Hor reigned for 13,420 years before Menes. Reigns before them — gods, the Venerables of Memphis, the Venerables of the North — totalled 23,200 years. Combined: 36,620 years.

Counting backward from Menes (c. 3,100 BC), the Shemsu-Hor would have begun ruling around 16,520 BC — before the Younger Dryas impact (c. 12,900 BC), not after it.

After ~12,900 BCSurvivors of both waves reach Earth, carrying the memory of that long reign

The resolution: the Shemsu-Hor did not rule Egypt. They ruled Secundus — the original civilisation of the Benei Elohim, with a circular genome and long lifespans, where a reign spanning millennia is entirely natural. It is a pre-impact reign, on their home planet, not a terrestrial government.

This explains why no physical monument, relief or artefact in Egypt depicts a "reign of the Shemsu-Hor" on Egyptian soil — because there wasn't one. What arrived in Egypt was the memory of that reign, carried by survivors who themselves never ruled there. They became, instead, the founders of what followed.

V
Why Only One Tradition Survived

Structured memory — the Ark's wave — transmits coherently because it originated in an organised group with intact leadership. Fragmented memory — Quinta's wave — dissolves into scattered, isolated myths without a unifying narrative.

It is the difference between a group that documents its history and one that only remembers it in pieces.
