Blue Blood

The Atlanteans and Haemocyanin

Venus · The Sangraal · An Inherited Biochemistry

The expression "blue blood" to describe royalty is much older than any knowledge of biochemistry. The official explanation is a metaphor about pale skin. There is another explanation. One that requires no metaphor.

The expression "blue blood" to describe royalty and nobility is much older than any knowledge of biochemistry. The official explanation is mundane: pale skin where veins show a bluish tint, attributed to the Spanish Visigothic nobility as opposed to the darker skin of the Moors.

But there is another explanation. One that requires no metaphor.

Haemocyanin is the protein that transports oxygen in the blood of octopuses, squids, spiders and horseshoe crabs. Unlike haemoglobin — which uses iron and gives our blood its red colour — haemocyanin uses copper. And copper-based blood, when oxygenated, is blue.

It is no coincidence that these animals share something more than the colour of their blood: they are organisms adapted to environments of low oxygen availability. And here lies the key that explains why.

Haemocyanin has an advantage that haemoglobin does not: it is not encapsulated in red blood cells with a fixed concentration. It floats freely in the haemolymph, and its concentration can increase according to need. An organism with haemocyanin can "manufacture more transporter" when oxygen becomes scarce, without redesigning its blood. It also retains oxygen better at low partial pressure — it is more efficient at capturing the few O2 molecules available in a poor environment.

On a planet with abundant atmospheric oxygen, that advantage is irrelevant: haemoglobin, with iron, transports oxygen far more efficiently when oxygen is plentiful. But on an oxygen-poor planet, the balance reverses.

A Marine Planet, Poor in Oxygen

If Venus was, as this project proposes, a predominantly oceanic planet — little land surface, little vegetation, and therefore little photosynthesis — its atmosphere would have had a much lower oxygen level than Earth's today. A world of water, with little green.

In that scenario, the life that evolved there did not merely have the option of developing haemocyanin: it had to. It was the optimal solution — perhaps the only viable one — for a planet where oxygen was the scarcest resource.

Blue blood. Not as a metaphor for purity. As evolution's answer to a planet that breathed little.

Amphibians in a World of Water

If the Atlanteans were amphibians — beings capable of moving between the ocean and Venus's scarce emerged land — they faced a double challenge: little oxygen in the water and little oxygen in the air. A fixed, inflexible oxygen-transport system, like haemoglobin in red blood cells, would not be the best option for someone constantly changing environments.

Haemocyanin, by contrast, allows oxygen-transport capacity to be adjusted according to the environment — more concentration when needed, without changing systems. For a Venusian amphibian, it would have been the natural evolutionary solution.

On Earth, our amphibians — frogs, salamanders — use haemoglobin, because they evolved on a planet with abundant oxygen. If the Atlanteans were amphibians with blue blood, they would not be an anomaly relative to Earth's evolution. They would be the expected result of evolution on a different planet — one that breathed less.

The Thin Air of the Heights

Andean traditions speak of amphibious beings who emerged from the waters of Lake Titicaca — at almost 4,000 metres of altitude, where the air contains barely 60% of the oxygen available at sea level — to bring knowledge to humanity. Viracocha and similar figures are not an isolated exception: multiple high-mountain traditions describe their civilising deities as beings who move naturally between water and an air that, for local humans, is already insufficient.

If those beings had blue blood — haemocyanin, capable of functioning efficiently both in oxygen-poor water and in thin air — choosing the heights as their point of arrival would be no coincidence. It would be the only place on Earth where their Venusian biochemistry found an environment similar to home.

Lake Titicaca — high altitude, cold, deep waters — may have been perceived by the Atlanteans not as a hostile place, but as the closest thing to home.

The Fauna That Came With Them

The Atlanteans did not arrive alone on Earth. They brought fauna with them. And that fauna, adapted by millions of years of evolution on an oxygen-poor planet, retained its blue blood even after colonising new terrestrial habitats. Octopuses. Squids. Arachnids. Primitive crustaceans.

The dynasties that claimed to have "royal blood" — Sangraal — were not entirely lying. Only no one understood the claim literally. Royal blood was not a metaphor for divine lineage. It was a description of a biochemistry inherited from a world that breathed differently.

Why It Almost Disappeared

Upon arriving on Earth, that inheritance encountered a very different planet: abundant atmospheric oxygen, thanks to a far more extensive plant biosphere than Venus's. And on an oxygen-rich planet, haemocyanin loses its advantage — haemoglobin, with iron, transports oxygen far more efficiently when oxygen is plentiful.

Natural selection did the rest. Blue blood survived only in the niches where oxygen remained scarce — the cold, oxygen-poor seafloor. The descendants who colonised the surface, breathing Earth's rich air, saw their blue blood displaced generation after generation by the more efficient red blood.

How many human lineages still carry traces of that inheritance? And why does haemocyanin — so useful on a planet that breathed little — survive only in a handful of organisms that, curiously, are the most "alien" on our planet?
Why Secundus and Quinta Were "Home"

If Earth — with its abundant vegetation and oxygen-rich atmosphere — was hostile to a mixed biochemistry adapted to a low-oxygen environment, then it is not that the gods "chose" Secundus and Quinta as their home worlds and Earth as a secondary colony. It is that Earth, as it was then, was not habitable for them — except in very specific niches: high mountains, cold seafloor, or through technology capable of reducing the oxygen they breathed.

This explains, all at once:
— Why they resided on Secundus/Quinta and not on Earth
— Why their Earth colonies are concentrated in high mountains (Titicaca, the Andes, the Tibetan plateaus)
— Why they needed Earth-born workers — beings already adapted to the oxygen-rich atmosphere — to operate on the surface on their behalf

Why Egypt — The Second Home

If the survivors of Secundus could choose anywhere on Earth to settle, the question is obvious: why Egypt?

The answer requires knowing what Egypt was when they arrived.

Between approximately 14,500 and 5,000 BC — the African Humid Period, documented by geology, palynology and sediment cores — the Sahara was not a desert. It was a landscape of permanent lakes, savannahs, rivers and dense vegetation. Elephants, giraffes and hippopotamuses in what is today the central Sahara. Abundant rainfall. A hot and very humid climate — monsoon expanded northward, Mediterranean moisture from the west.

Precisely the climate of an oceanic planet with little emerged land and a water-saturated atmosphere.

They did not choose Egypt despite the environment. They chose it because it was the place on Earth that most resembled Secundus.

The birth of Egyptian civilisation is directly linked by science to this period. When the African Humid Period ended — in some areas in as little as 100 to 200 years, around 5,000 BC — the last niche on Earth compatible with their biochemistry disappeared.

The dilution was genetic through interbreeding — generation after generation of reproduction with humans. There was no adaptive pressure, no biological response to climate change.

What did occur was simpler: they tried to slow the genetic dilution through endogamy. The marriages between siblings among the pharaohs were not perversion or cultural custom. They were the desperate attempt to preserve the haemocyanin component in the royal lineage.

But endogamy could not withstand the generations. The dilution was inevitable.

The Mask That Survived

There is an object that appears in every representation of the pharaoh — alive or dead — and yet it is not what it appears to be.

The official explanation calls it a false beard. But pharaohs were rigorously shaved. Not a single hair on their real faces. What they wore was the postiche: a rigid piece of metal — gold, the most prestigious material, sometimes bronze or precious stone — attached to the face by a strap that looped around the ears and tied at the back of the head, connected to a gold band sealed against the chin.

If this were a decorative symbol, why metal? Why rigid? Why a mechanical fastening system designed for tight contact with the skin? A painted beard on the crown would have served the same symbolic purpose. You use straps and a sealed chin band when the object needs hermetic contact with the face.

The academic explanation does not survive scrutiny. It explains the what but not the how. It does not explain the rigid metal, the chin band, the exclusivity, or the different design for the gods.

The academy does not answer:
— Why rigid metal and not cloth?
— Why a strap system for hermetic skin contact?
— Why exclusive to the pharaoh, with a different design for the gods?
— Why does it disappear in 1580 BC without decree or explanation?

Unless the function is not symbolic.

The Chronology the Academy Does Not Interpret

Old Kingdom (~2686–2181 BC): short, square-ended. Maximum coverage of nose and mouth. Hermetic chamber, efficient.

Middle Kingdom (~2055–1650 BC): lengthens, slight curve. Less nasal coverage. Chamber less hermetic.

New Kingdom (~1550–1070 BC): long, braided, curved upward. Minimum functional coverage. The object has become jewellery.

After 1580 BC: disappears without explanation.

The progressive genetic dilution of the haemocyanin component made the hermetic chamber increasingly unnecessary. When the physiological need disappears, the device becomes a symbol. When dilution is complete, the symbol has no reason to exist.

The device was not abandoned by cultural decision. It was abandoned because the body that needed it had ceased to exist.

The Biochemistry of the Problem

The inhabitants of Secundus did not necessarily have haemocyanin instead of haemoglobin. They may have had both — simultaneously. A mixed blood system calibrated for the same low oxygen range.

On Earth, too much oxygen for that mixed system. The haemocyanin component — evolved for maximum efficiency in scarcity — saturated in abundance. Two systems pulling in different directions. Permanent low-level stress.

The solution is the same principle as breathing into a paper bag.

When a person hyperventilates, they expel too much CO₂ and blood pH rises. Breathing into a paper bag restores the balance by reintroducing exhaled air, partially depleted of O₂ and enriched with CO₂.

The postiche works by the same physical mechanism — for a different biochemistry. Rigid metal sealed against the nose and mouth creates a small chamber. Each exhalation depletes the O₂ in that chamber. Each inhalation draws from air slightly richer in CO₂ and poorer in O₂. Not enough to affect a pure haemoglobin system. Precisely enough to bring the mixed system back into equilibrium.

Not a breathing mask in the modern sense. A biochemical regulator.

Why Only the Pharaoh

The pharaoh retained enough haemocyanin component to experience the imbalance. The ordinary Egyptian, with purely haemoglobin-based blood, felt nothing.

The gods with the curved variant carried purer Secundus blood. Different haemocyanin concentration, different chamber geometry. Not stylistic variation. Technical specification.

What every museum in the world displays as a false beard is a biochemical regulator for a mixed-blood organism from a planet that breathed differently.

The Trace in Our Own DNA

If the Atlanteans' descendants developed red blood without any new mutation — simply rebalancing the expression of two genes that already coexisted since Venus — this raises three specific questions:

1. Could there be remnants — silenced genes or pseudogenes — of haemocyanin in the human genome, homologous to those of octopuses, squids or horseshoe crabs?

2. Could there be structural remnants of the circular genome configuration (the Shen symbol, protected telomeres) in the "non-coding" regions of human DNA — the so-called junk DNA that current genetics still doesn't fully explain?

3. In organisms where both genes coexist, can there be partial simultaneous expression — true "mixed blood" — that might persist today in some human lineage?

We are not claiming it exists. We are asking whether anyone has looked.
The Animal They Brought With Them
🔴 DECLARED SPECULATION

The organisms with haemocyanin on Earth are precisely those that most perplex evolutionary biologists: octopuses, squids, spiders, horseshoe crabs. Not only for their biochemistry. For everything else.

The octopus:
— Two thirds of its neurons distributed across its tentacles. A neural architecture with no parallel in any other terrestrial animal.
— Three hearts. Blue blood. Eyes practically identical in structure to human eyes — but evolved completely independently.
— 33,000 genes — more than the human being — with real-time RNA editing that no other known organism possesses.
— In 2018, a paper in Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology, signed by thirty-three researchers, suggested that squid eggs could have arrived from space frozen in comets. It was ridiculed. But the paper exists, and nobody has refuted its content with an argument that is not ridicule.

If the survivors of Secundus brought fauna with them, they would have brought the organisms they knew, that shared their biochemistry, adapted to the same low-oxygen environment. The pets of a civilisation that breathed differently.

What we today call the most intelligent animal in the ocean may be the pet of the gods.

Why does the most "alien" animal on the planet have the same blood as the royalty of every ancient culture?
