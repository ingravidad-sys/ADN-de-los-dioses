Sodom and Gomorrah

The purge of a failed experiment?

Verified chronology, declared reinterpretation

This essay builds on the framework developed in "The Fear-of-God Cluster" — the hypothesis that obedience/religiosity has a real, documented genetic component (Koenig & Bouchard 2006, heritability 40-60%). Here we ask what the narrated destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah would mean within that framework.
✅ PART I — THE REAL ARCHAEOLOGICAL CHRONOLOGY (AND ITS PROBLEM)

I.1 — Two candidate sites, two different chronologies

There is no archaeological consensus on the location of Sodom and Gomorrah. Two candidates compete with chronologies that don't match:

SiteLocationDestruction datingPhysical evidence
Tall el-HammamNorth of the Dead Sea~1650 BCETemperatures above 2,500°C, melted pottery, carbon microdiamonds — consistent with an airburst event (comet/meteorite)
Bab edh-Dhra / NumeiraSoutheast of the Dead Sea~2067-2100 BCECharnel houses burned from the roof downward — a fire pattern consistent with burning material falling from above

I.2 — The unresolved chronological problem

If Sodom is placed in the time of Abraham (traditional biblical chronology, roughly 2166-1806 BCE), Tall el-Hammam is 300-400 years too late — its destruction (1650 BCE) postdates the period the Bible places Abraham in. Bab edh-Dhra and Numeira fit the biblical framework chronologically better, but lack the extreme thermal evidence Tall el-Hammam presents.

Rigor note: the 2021 study linking Tall el-Hammam's destruction to a cosmic impact (published in Scientific Reports) was retracted by the journal in 2025 after methodological review. Any reading using that study as definitive proof of a cosmic event should be treated with due caution — the data of destruction by extreme fire at both sites remains real, but the exact cause (cosmic impact vs. another mechanism) is not settled.
🔴 PART II — A REINTERPRETATION (DECLARED HYPOTHESIS)
Methodological note: what follows connects directly to the hypothesis developed in "The Fear-of-God Cluster" — that obedience/religiosity is a trait with a real genetic component, potentially inserted and reinforced in specific populations. This is not the position of archaeology or academic genetics.

II.1 — Two cities, two failures

The biblical account doesn't describe the destruction of a single "sinful" city, but of two twin cities (plus three lesser ones according to Deuteronomy 29:22-23), destroyed simultaneously, in the same region, by the same event. If the "sin" the text punishes is, ultimately, the absence of the obedience/fear trait that the rest of the biblical narrative treats as the marker of a chosen lineage, the existence of two neighboring, contemporary populations both purged at once fits the idea of an insertion experiment that failed in more than one place simultaneously, not in one isolated site.

II.1.b — The chronological gap, resolved: not the first wave

This project places the original genetic deployment of Homo sapiens around 300,000 years ago (see "The Built Link") — a gap of nearly three thousand centuries compared to the archaeological dating of Sodom and Gomorrah (Early/Middle Bronze Age, only 3,650-4,100 years ago). That time gap would be incompatible with the idea that Sodom was "the original experiment failing" — no coherent insertion program would take 296,000 years to attempt correcting a single failure.

The resolution fits the framework already developed in "The Two Waves": the deployment 300,000 years ago was the first wave — the generic installation of the trait across the entire species. Sodom and Gomorrah, by contrast, belong to the second wave — the much later arrival of survivors after the collapse of Secundus/Quinta (the event this project identifies with the Flood), settling in the Canaan/Mesopotamia region in recent historical time. It isn't the original humanity failing at Sodom — it's a group of recent arrivals, or their immediate descendants, being evaluated separately.

This also explains the geography: Sodom and Gomorrah are located exactly where this project already places the second wave's post-flood settlement — not a coincidence, but narrative continuity within the same framework.

II.2 — The prior warning as a verification protocol, not mercy

The account includes a step rarely read closely: before the destruction, messengers are sent to explicitly check whether at least a minimum number of "righteous" individuals exist in the city (Genesis 18:22-33, Abraham's bargaining over ten righteous). Read as protocol, not moral drama, this is exactly the step one would expect from a quality-control check before purging an entire population: verifying whether the target trait (obedience/fear) took hold in any individual before proceeding to eliminate the rest.

II.3 — The salt as a marker, not symbolic punishment

Lot's wife turns into a pillar of salt after disobeying the explicit instruction not to look back (Genesis 19:26) — a punishment that, read literally, is disproportionate to the infraction (a glance) if interpreted as moral judgment. Read as protocol, however, it fits the idea of an operational safety rule broken during an evacuation from an active purge zone — the consequence isn't punishment for sin, it's what happens when someone breaks exit protocol during an ongoing process.

The question this opens: if "fear of God" is, as the previous essay in this project proposes, a trait with a real genetic component that was attempted to be inserted and reinforced in specific populations, does the account of Sodom and Gomorrah describe the purge of two pilot populations where the trait failed to take — with Lot and his family as the only verified carriers, the reason for their prior extraction before the destruction?
🟣 THE OPEN QUESTION

None of this reinterpretation replaces the real archaeological chronology of Part I, which remains academically unresolved. What Part II proposes is a different question: if the biblical account systematically narrates the selection and purging of populations according to an obedience trait transmitted by lineage, does the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah record, in mythic language, the elimination of two batches from an experiment that didn't reach the expected result?

No closed answer is offered. We invite falsification.
